Method

Order of authority

  1. Via negativa first. Remove what can cause ruin before looking at upside: company debt beyond the sector's trough tolerance, weak interest cover, owner debt (pledge) beyond the sector's sensitivity, chronic negative CFO, serial dilution, audit and governance flags. For CDMO, defence and aerospace, also his valuation cut-off — P/S above 15 and market cap above 400× depreciation together — and, for science and tech, negative CFO with market cap above 200× depreciation. P/S above 15 alone is flagged, not removed. Finally, a rich valuation on a fragile system (negative divergence) is rejected.
  2. The system. Resilience (balance sheet against sector tolerance, cash conversion, concentration, margin stability, capitalised-R&D fragility), trajectory (3-year change in ROCE, gross margin, EBITDA margin, receivable days, plus the quarterly hurdle), moat and optionality (Thiel's four plus diversity of customers, suppliers and products — Greene's dependency and bargaining power — and whether the capability carries over to other sectors), and management (skin in the game, delivery against guidance, red flags, whether they blame outside factors).
  3. Valuation. P/S, EV/EBITDA and the growth the price implies (reverse DCF) against an approximate base rate. Plotted against the system: that's the SV map.
  4. Net margin of safety = the minimum of business resilience, management and valuation cheapness. It's an intersection, so one weak leg sinks it.
  5. Peers. Screener's peer table for the same industry (top listed peers plus the median of every company in it). P/E against the median makes up 20% of the valuation score (Settings); ROCE against the median moves the moat score by up to ±1. A premium with below-median ROCE is called out: it pays for a future, not today's returns.
  6. Rate of change. Sales growth YoY for each of the last four quarters; acceleration = the latest two minus the two before. Labels: Accelerating, Decelerating, Inflecting up, Rolling over, Steady. Together with operating leverage it makes up 20% of the trajectory score. Accelerations mean-revert, so the base-rate expectations model is unchanged.
  7. Operating leverage. Semi-fixed costs (employee, manufacturing, other, depreciation) as % of sales; incremental margin over 3 years (Δ operating profit ÷ Δ sales) against the base margin; DOL; asset turn against its 5-year best (spare capacity). High fixed costs with falling sales is flagged as concave (and costs 0.5 resilience in cyclical sectors).
  8. Capabilities and what the company is getting into. A capability map per company: what it has (technologies, chemistries, modalities, markets) and what it is getting into, each with a target market, an evidence stage (0 none · 1 announced · 2 capex committed · 3 plant built / qualifying · 4 first commercial revenue · 5 >10% of revenue, counted 0/10/30/50/80/100%) and adjacency (extension of a core capability 100%, adjacent 80%, new unrelated field 40% — far moves fail more often). It starts from capabilities detected in the company description and is edited by you or the optional Claude draft. Capabilities rare across the scanned market count more. The getting-into score feeds the option score (CONVEX), never the margin of safety. A revenue-due date that passes before stage 4 becomes a time trigger.
  9. Capability peers. Peers are companies sharing capabilities and markets (overlap = shared ÷ combined), from your list and the market scan. For each thing a company is getting into, the engine lists who is already there (incumbents, with P/E, ROCE, growth) and who else is entering. Screener's industry table is kept as a secondary check.
  10. Terminal value from capabilities and people. After the forecast years the engine no longer assumes excess returns forever. Capabilities and people (management score) set how many more years the company keeps earning its ROCE (up to 30, Settings); after that ROCE falls to the cost of capital and growth adds nothing. It also solves the reverse question — how many years of advantage the price needs at base-rate growth — and the gap between the two is part of the valuation score.
  11. People, measured by outcomes. The people score is skin in the game (35%), governance (35%) and the capital-allocation record (30%). Allocation is computed: return on the capital added over 5 years (Δ EBIT ÷ Δ capital employed, excluding work in progress) against the cost of equity, and capex payoff (sales per rupee of new net block ÷ the old asset turn). Promises you log in the Journal, once two or more are resolved, replace the guidance-record input. Promoter/KMP pay above 5% / 10% of net profit, a credit-rating downgrade and missing succession all count against it.
  12. Credit ratings. The server reads the latest rating report linked on Screener (ICRA PDFs, CRISIL/CARE pages): grade, outlook, action, the agency's liquidity view, and what it says about customer concentration and related parties. The grade nudges resilience by up to ±0.5; "stretched" or "poor" liquidity costs 0.5; a customer share quoted by the agency is used when you have entered none. A withdrawn rating falls back to the other agency's.
  13. Base rates by size and sector. Growth expectations use the spread of forward 3–5-year sales growth of listed companies of similar starting revenue (and sector, where there are 40+ cases), from the history replay, instead of one number for every company. Survivors only, so read them as optimistic. Switch off in Settings.
  14. History replay. For every scanned company the scanner re-scores each past financial year as of 4 months after it closed, using only figures published by then, and records the next 1- and 3-year returns and growth. The Signal Lab tab reports returns by bucket, which parts of the engine predicted returns (rank correlation per year, t-statistic, earlier vs later years) and whether P(beat) is calibrated. Delisted companies are missing and judgement inputs are neutral in the replay.
  15. Portfolio risk, costs and tax. Shared exposure by sector, capability and market (cap 35%), how many independent bets the positions amount to given their return correlations, days to exit at 20% of daily traded value, and the tax and costs of selling (STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5% after 12 months, FY 2026-27 rates), including what waiting for long-term treatment saves.
  16. Journal and alerts. Each decision is logged with your thesis, what would prove it wrong, a pre-mortem, your confidence and the engine's call at that moment; reviews score whether you were right, whether your overrides beat the engine, and how well your confidence is calibrated (Brier score). The list, journal and promises are kept on your Supabase. Every morning the server checks for quarterly hurdle misses, falls from your cost, entry zones, overdue milestones and revenue dates, rating downgrades, and reviews or promises due — shown on the page and optionally pushed to your phone via ntfy.
  17. Customers. Largest-customer share, top-5 share or a spread judgement (0 one customer >50% … 3 broad) carry 3 of the 10 resilience points. One customer above 50%: resilience capped at 5 and never CORE (CONVEX at most).
  18. Capital-lag stage. Low ROCE with a strong balance sheet, healthy gross margin and capacity still being built is "passing pain", valued on a normalised ROCE instead of being penalised. Very high ROCE in a cyclical sector is flagged as a likely peak.
  19. Crowding (six signs). Re-rated multiple, expectations priced in, hot narrative, bullish sell-side, institutions trimming while retail piles in, and a missed quarterly hurdle. When enough line up at once, the action is TRIM, not add.
  20. Regret profile. "Not owning enough": staged buying (20% / 20–30% / final 50% on evidence), larger caps, price falls trigger investigation, not selling. "Overpaying": buy only when valuation is undemanding, smaller caps, a hard price stop.
  21. Kill switch for held positions: price → investigate, time → reassess at the milestone date you set, thesis broken → exit.
  22. Sizing. Barbell: safe side, core (cap × net margin of safety ÷ 10, with fractional Kelly on base-rate scenarios shown as a cross-check) and convex (small fixed stakes sized by maximum loss). Sector caps so one sector can't force you out of another. Tested price signals can halve weak names.

Honest limits

  • His numbers are qualitative; mine are not. The sector table rates debt tolerance and pledge sensitivity in words; the numeric cut-offs here are my translation and are editable. The SV axes are my composite scores — he draws the curve by judgement.
  • Only the price signals are tested. The system score is a snapshot and can't be backtested without point-in-time fundamentals history.
  • Base rates are approximate (μ 10%, σ 8% for 10-year revenue CAGR, Indian nominal). Replace with your own.
  • Not Medallion. The Simons part is the discipline of testing, not his methods.
  • His video and audio commentary isn't encoded; only the written notes and slides.
  • A screening and sizing aid, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor — and he repeatedly says not to clone anyone's portfolio, including his.
