Method

Order of authority

  1. Via negativa first. Remove what can cause ruin before looking at upside: company debt beyond the sector's trough tolerance, weak interest cover, owner debt (pledge) beyond the sector's sensitivity, chronic negative CFO, serial dilution, audit and governance flags. For CDMO, defence and aerospace, also his valuation cut-off — P/S above 15 and market cap above 400× depreciation together — and, for science and tech, negative CFO with market cap above 200× depreciation. P/S above 15 alone is flagged, not removed. Finally, a rich valuation on a fragile system (negative divergence) is rejected.
  2. The system. Resilience (balance sheet against sector tolerance, cash conversion, concentration, margin stability, capitalised-R&D fragility), trajectory (3-year change in ROCE, gross margin, EBITDA margin, receivable days, plus the quarterly hurdle), moat and optionality (Thiel's four plus diversity of customers, suppliers and products — Greene's dependency and bargaining power — and whether the capability carries over to other sectors), and management (skin in the game, delivery against guidance, red flags, whether they blame outside factors).
  3. Valuation. P/S, EV/EBITDA and the growth the price implies (reverse DCF) against an approximate base rate. Plotted against the system: that's the SV map.
  4. Net margin of safety = the minimum of business resilience, management and valuation cheapness. It's an intersection, so one weak leg sinks it.
  5. Capital-lag stage. Low ROCE with a strong balance sheet, healthy gross margin and capacity still being built is "passing pain", valued on a normalised ROCE instead of being penalised. Very high ROCE in a cyclical sector is flagged as a likely peak.
  6. Crowding (six signs). Re-rated multiple, expectations priced in, hot narrative, bullish sell-side, institutions trimming while retail piles in, and a missed quarterly hurdle. When enough line up at once, the action is TRIM, not add.
  7. Regret profile. "Not owning enough": staged buying (20% / 20–30% / final 50% on evidence), larger caps, price falls trigger investigation, not selling. "Overpaying": buy only when valuation is undemanding, smaller caps, a hard price stop.
  8. Kill switch for held positions: price → investigate, time → reassess at the milestone date you set, thesis broken → exit.
  9. Sizing. Barbell: safe side, core (cap × net margin of safety ÷ 10, with fractional Kelly on base-rate scenarios shown as a cross-check) and convex (small fixed stakes sized by maximum loss). Sector caps so one sector can't force you out of another. Tested price signals can halve weak names.

Honest limits

  • His numbers are qualitative; mine are not. The sector table rates debt tolerance and pledge sensitivity in words; the numeric cut-offs here are my translation and are editable. The SV axes are my composite scores — he draws the curve by judgement.
  • Only the price signals are tested. The system score is a snapshot and can't be backtested without point-in-time fundamentals history.
  • Base rates are approximate (μ 10%, σ 8% for 10-year revenue CAGR, Indian nominal). Replace with your own.
  • Not Medallion. The Simons part is the discipline of testing, not his methods.
  • His video and audio commentary isn't encoded; only the written notes and slides.
  • A screening and sizing aid, not investment advice. I'm not a financial advisor — and he repeatedly says not to clone anyone's portfolio, including his.
